SCIENTIFIC BACKGROUND

Research

Understanding the biomechanics of ankle injury and rehabilitation is central to the development of GaitMate. This section documents the scientific and clinical research informing the project.

The Clinical Problem

Lateral ankle sprains are common musculoskeletal injuries involving structures that stabilize the lateral ankle. Rehabilitation aims to restore mobility, strength, balance, and functional movement while reducing the risk of recurrent injury.

Lateral Ankle Anatomy

GaitMate currently focuses on the lateral ligament complex of the ankle, including the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL), calcaneofibular ligament (CFL), and posterior talofibular ligament (PTFL).

Why Wearable Sensing?

Wearable technologies create opportunities to collect movement-related measurements outside traditional clinical environments. GaitMate explores how localized wearable measurements could complement rehabilitation assessments by providing longitudinal information about movement during recovery.

Research Questions

01

Can wearable sensors reliably measure localized mechanical changes around the ankle during movement?

02

Which measurements are most informative for characterizing changes during rehabilitation?

03

How can sensor measurements be translated into useful and understandable information for patients and clinicians?
