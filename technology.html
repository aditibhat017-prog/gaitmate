TECHNOLOGY

Connecting wearable sensing with real-time feedback.

GaitMate combines a wearable biosensing sticker concept with a digital rehabilitation platform to investigate how movement and biomechanical measurements can support more personalized injury recovery.

Our hardware and software are being developed iteratively, beginning with localized ankle monitoring and expanding toward broader gait analysis applications.

HARDWARE

Designing a wearable sensing system.

GaitMate's hardware is currently under development. Our initial design investigates multiple sensing locations around the lateral ankle to capture localized biomechanical changes during movement.

We are exploring several sensor technologies, including piezoresistive strain sensors, capacitive stretch sensors, inertial measurement units (IMUs), and electromyography (EMG). Each modality offers different information about deformation, motion, or muscle activity.

01

Piezoresistive Strain Sensors

Measure changes in electrical resistance caused by mechanical deformation. These sensors may provide a flexible approach to localized stretch monitoring.

02

Capacitive Stretch Sensors

Measure deformation through changes in capacitance. Their flexible structure makes them a potential candidate for wearable movement sensing.

03

Inertial Measurement Units

Combine accelerometer and gyroscope measurements, sometimes with magnetometers, to characterize movement, acceleration, and orientation.

04

Electromyography (EMG)

Measures electrical activity associated with muscle activation. EMG may provide complementary information about neuromuscular function.

WEARABLE BIOSENSING STICKER

A compact, three-layer design.

Our proposed wearable sticker is designed around a hydrocolloid-patch-sized format, with three functional layers intended to support sensing during rehabilitation.

TOP LAYER

Protective Exterior

A waterproof covering intended to protect the sensing components from environmental exposure during everyday use.

MIDDLE LAYER

Sensing Mechanism

Contains the selected sensor technology and associated connections used to capture movement or localized biomechanical measurements.

BOTTOM LAYER

Skin-Safe Adhesive

An adhesive interface intended to maintain sensor placement while accommodating movement and minimizing skin irritation.

INITIAL SENSOR CONFIGURATION

Nine sensing locations around the lateral ankle.

Our initial ankle rehabilitation concept proposes nine sensing locations, organized into three groups associated with the major lateral ankle ligament regions.

Three sensors are proposed near each ligament region to investigate how measurements vary spatially during movement. The configuration is a research design, and the ability to infer ligament strain from skin-mounted sensors has not yet been established.

03 SENSORS

ATFL Region

Anterior talofibular ligament region. An important focus in lateral ankle sprain research.

03 SENSORS

CFL Region

Calcaneofibular ligament region. Included to investigate localized mechanical behavior around the lateral ankle.

03 SENSORS

PTFL Region

Posterior talofibular ligament region. Included in the proposed multi-location sensing architecture.

SOFTWARE

Turning sensor measurements into useful feedback.

GaitMate's initial app design was scaffolded during the UCSF QBI Hackathon. The prototype explores how movement and rehabilitation information could be displayed through an accessible digital interface.

Our software development roadmap includes real-time strain visualization, movement-event detection, rehabilitation progress tracking, and personalized exercise guidance.

01

Color-Coded Strain Scale

A proposed three-level visualization communicates relative strain or loading categories:

Green — Low
Yellow — Moderate
Red — High

Thresholds in the early demo are informed by literature-based assumptions about ankle ligament strain and loading. They require experimental calibration and validation before being interpreted as patient-specific risk.

02

Fall Detection

Planned movement-event detection aims to identify patterns consistent with falls or instability and support timely feedback.

Longer-term research will investigate whether changes in gait, balance, and movement variability could contribute to estimating fall risk before an event occurs.

03

Physical Therapy Exercises

A proposed exercise-guidance feature would organize rehabilitation activities using recovery stage, injury history, and movement measurements.

Future versions would require clinician-reviewed exercise protocols and validation before providing individualized therapeutic recommendations.

DATA & ANALYTICS

From raw signals to movement insights.

Our planned analysis pipeline connects wearable measurements with interpretable indicators of movement and recovery.

01 — CAPTURE

Sensor Signals

Record movement-related signals such as localized deformation, acceleration, orientation, or muscle activity, depending on the selected sensors.

02 — ANALYZE

Movement Features

Explore signal changes, asymmetry, variability, and movement patterns across activities and time.

03 — DISPLAY

Digital Feedback

Present movement information through visual summaries, progress trends, and proposed feedback categories for patients and clinicians.

The current demo uses prototype or illustrative data. Hardware integration, clinical interpretation, and real-time performance are ongoing development goals.
