IMPACT

From recovery to long-term mobility.

GaitMate began with a simple question: how can wearable technology help us better understand recovery outside of the clinic?

Our current work focuses on ankle rehabilitation, while the broader platform creates opportunities to explore how wearable sensing could support movement, mobility, and rehabilitation across different populations.

CURRENT FOCUS

Ankle Injury Rehabilitation

Ankle rehabilitation does not stop when a patient leaves the clinic. Much of recovery occurs during everyday movement, exercise, and rehabilitation activities performed independently.

GaitMate is being developed to explore whether wearable sensors can capture localized mechanical information around the ankle and translate those measurements into useful feedback throughout rehabilitation.

01

Monitor

Explore ways to measure movement and mechanical changes during rehabilitation activities.

02

Understand

Transform wearable sensor measurements into information that is easier to interpret.

03

Support

Investigate how longitudinal information could complement rehabilitation and clinical decision-making.

FUTURE DIRECTIONS

Beyond ankle rehabilitation.

GaitMate's broader vision is to investigate how distributed wearable sensing could eventually support other areas of rehabilitation and mobility. These applications represent potential directions for future research and have not yet been clinically validated.

01

Athletic Rehabilitation

Future systems could explore movement and recovery across different sports, training environments, and musculoskeletal injuries.

02

Healthy Aging & Mobility

Wearable movement sensing could be investigated as a way to better understand changes in mobility, balance, and functional movement over time.

03

Neurological Rehabilitation

Future research could investigate whether wearable sensing can help characterize movement patterns during neurological rehabilitation.

04

Cardiovascular Recovery

Future versions of the platform could explore integration with physiological sensors to study the relationship between movement, exertion, and recovery.

05

Musculoskeletal Injuries

The sensing framework could eventually be adapted to investigate rehabilitation beyond the ankle, including other joints and movement-related injuries.

+

A Platform for Exploration

Rather than designing GaitMate around a single measurement, the long-term goal is to build a flexible wearable platform that can evolve alongside new research questions.

LONG-TERM VISION

Making recovery visible.

Our long-term vision is to bridge the gap between structured clinical evaluation and the movement that happens throughout everyday life.
