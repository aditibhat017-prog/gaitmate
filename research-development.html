RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT

Building GaitMate.

GaitMate combines rehabilitation research, wearable sensing, engineering design, and digital analysis to explore new ways of understanding movement throughout recovery.

Follow the project from the clinical problem and initial concept through prototype development, testing, and future validation.

01 — CLINICAL MOTIVATION

Recovery extends beyond the clinic.

Rehabilitation is often evaluated through periodic clinical assessments, while much of a patient's recovery occurs between appointments during everyday movement, exercise, and independent rehabilitation.

GaitMate explores whether wearable sensing can provide additional information about movement during these periods and help create a more continuous picture of rehabilitation.

02 — ANKLE ANATOMY

Starting with lateral ankle injuries.

GaitMate's initial research focuses on structures involved in lateral ankle sprains. The early sensor concept investigates localized sensing around three major lateral ankle ligaments.

ATFL

Anterior talofibular ligament.

CFL

Calcaneofibular ligament.

PTFL

Posterior talofibular ligament.

03 — RESEARCH QUESTION

Can wearable sensors help characterize localized mechanical changes during ankle rehabilitation?

This question guides the development of the GaitMate sensing system, data-analysis approach, and digital interface.

04 — DESIGN GOALS

Turning a research question into a wearable system.

The design process considers both the technical requirements of sensing and the practical requirements of a device intended to operate during movement.

01

Localized

Explore measurements at multiple locations around the ankle rather than relying on a single sensing point.

02

Wearable

Develop a system that can ultimately integrate with movement and rehabilitation activities.

03

Interpretable

Translate sensor measurements into information that can be understood more easily than raw sensor output.

04

Longitudinal

Investigate changes across rehabilitation rather than treating movement as a single isolated measurement.

05 — DEVELOPMENT

From concept to validation.

GaitMate is being developed iteratively. Each stage informs the next generation of the system.

01

Concept & Research

Define the clinical problem, initial sensing approach, system architecture, and research questions.

02

Prototype Development

Translate the proposed wearable system into physical hardware and software prototypes.

03

Bench Testing

Characterize sensor response, repeatability, mechanical behavior, and system performance under controlled conditions.

04

Wearable Integration

Evaluate how sensing components, adhesives, electronics, and software operate together as a wearable system.

05

Future Validation

Future research will be needed to determine whether the system produces meaningful and reliable measurements during rehabilitation.

06 — CURRENT DEVELOPMENT

What we're working on now.

Current development focuses on moving GaitMate beyond the conceptual design stage and establishing the technical foundations required for a functional prototype.

Sensor Prototyping

Investigating sensing approaches and physical configurations for measuring mechanical changes around the ankle.

Wearable Design

Developing the patch structure, placement strategy, and integration of sensing components into a wearable format.

Data Analysis

Developing methods for processing sensor measurements and identifying useful movement features.

Digital Platform

Designing the software interface that will eventually connect sensor measurements with interpretable rehabilitation feedback.

07 — TESTING STRATEGY

How will we know it works?

Before investigating clinical applications, the sensing system must first demonstrate reliable technical performance.

Sensor Response

Characterize how sensor output changes under controlled mechanical deformation.

Repeatability

Determine whether similar mechanical conditions produce consistent measurements across repeated trials.

Wearability

Investigate practical considerations including placement, movement, adhesion, comfort, and integration.

08 — LIMITATIONS

An early-stage research platform.

GaitMate is currently an early-stage research and engineering project. The system has not been clinically validated and is not intended for diagnosis, treatment, or medical decision-making.

Future work will require technical characterization, iterative prototype development, and appropriate validation before claims about clinical performance can be made.

09 — REFERENCES

Research foundation.

The scientific literature informing GaitMate includes research on lateral ankle injuries, rehabilitation, biomechanics, wearable sensing, and movement monitoring.

A complete reference list will be added as the research review and prototype development progress.
